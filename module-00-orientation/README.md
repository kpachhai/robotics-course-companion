# Module 0 - Orientation

**Goal:** build an accurate mental map of robotics before touching any math: what a robot actually is, what it is physically built from, what its sensors literally return, the five ways robots get programmed, why the field turned to learning, how to place any result on the map, where things stand in 2026, and exactly how your next 12 months are structured.

**Prerequisites:** none. **Time:** ~4 hours of reading, 6-8 hours with the exercises (one week at course pace).

## Lessons

1. [What a robot actually is](https://robotics.biblio.guru/course/orientation/what-a-robot-is/) - the sense-decide-act loop, joints and degrees of freedom, and the anatomy of the arm you'll build.
2. [A short history of robots: why each era ended](https://robotics.biblio.guru/course/orientation/a-short-history-of-robots/) - robots go where the uncertainty has already been paid for, and what happened every time someone left the factory.
3. [The machine itself: how electricity becomes motion](https://robotics.biblio.guru/course/orientation/the-machine-itself/) - inside a servo, what a gearbox costs you, and why position control breaks eggs.
4. [What sensing actually returns](https://robotics.biblio.guru/course/orientation/what-sensing-actually-returns/) - the literal arrays from camera, encoder, IMU and force sensor, their units, rates and dominant errors.
5. [How robots are programmed](https://robotics.biblio.guru/course/orientation/how-robots-are-programmed/) - five paradigms from scripted motion to foundation models, the artifact each one makes you author, and how each fails.
6. [Why the field turned to learning](https://robotics.biblio.guru/course/orientation/why-the-field-turned-to-learning/) - why hand-written rules stopped scaling for perception and behaviour, and which layers never moved.
7. [The map of the field](https://robotics.biblio.guru/course/orientation/the-map-of-the-field/) - four axes that place any paper, product or module of this course.
8. [The 2026 landscape: demonstrated versus announced](https://robotics.biblio.guru/course/orientation/the-2026-landscape/) - who has actually put robots to work, who has announced it, and the four questions that separate them.
9. [Your path through this course](https://robotics.biblio.guru/course/orientation/your-path-through-this-course/) - the module map, the weekly rhythm, and how to study with an AI assistant.

## Milestone (small, on purpose)

Write a one-page note in your own words: *"What I believe about robotics in 2026 and what I'm betting on."* Include: which layer of the stack you're targeting and why, one thing that surprised you from these lessons, and one claim you're skeptical of. Commit it as `notes/00-thesis.md`. You'll revisit it at Module 7 and enjoy being wrong about parts of it.

## Check-out test

You can explain to a colleague, without notes: what a degree of freedom is; why a servo commanded by angle is dangerous on contact; what a camera frame literally contains; why imitation learning took over from hand-coded motion for manipulation; and why "the demo worked" and "the robot works" are different claims.
